What the council is

The Retail Product Council is a multi-agent system that runs inside Claude Code, in a terminal or in VS Code. You bring a problem, an idea or a proposal. A Product Lead orchestrator frames it, checks what the knowledge base already knows, asks you only what it cannot answer, calls the panels that matter, makes them challenge each other, and ends with a short decision, an iteration path and, if you want one, a clean analysis document.

It is a base for all Thoughtworks retail work. The base holds what is true across retail: the areas, the experts, the reference players, the case studies, the failures and the vocabulary. Each client project starts from a copy of it and adds its own product.

What it is not

It does not replace user research, and its simulated users produce hypotheses to test rather than findings. It does not own decisions. People do. In the base it knows retail, not your product, and it says so when a fact is missing rather than inventing one.

How a decision gets made

Six steps from your question to a decision you can defend. Simple questions stop after the first answer.

  1. You askA question, a problem, a proposal or a file to ingest.
  2. The orchestrator looks it up, then asksIt separates problem from solution and looks up everything your request names, in the knowledge base, past sessions and the global case studies. It tells you what it already knows, then asks only about the gaps, each question with a default.
  3. Panels answer, then ask backEach panel thinks in its own context and judges the idea for shoppers, the business and stakeholders. Their questions and the figures they need are checked against the knowledge base, and only what it cannot answer comes back to you.
  4. Disagreements are forced into the openPanels that conflict get each other's argument and must hold, revise or concede.
  5. You get a short decisionTwo or three different approaches, each with a verdict, one named pick, an iteration path from the smallest viable version, what data would flip it, and a diagram when the pick changes how something works.
  6. The system learnsCorrections become rules. Decisions become records whose outcomes you track later. What a project learns about retail flows back to the base.

Why you can trust the answer

The council is only as good as its evidence, so it is strict about where every statement comes from.

  1. Every fact has a sourceA claim the decision rests on carries the source's own words, and a check confirms each quote really appears in the file it cites.
  2. It says when it does not knowA missing fact is reported as "not in context", never filled with a guess. In the base, product facts stay "to confirm" until a project fills them.
  3. Every case names its regionA solution from another market comes with its region and a transfer check, because density, labour cost, payment rails and regulation differ.
  4. Simulated means simulatedUser Panel answers are hypotheses to test with real users, and always labelled.
