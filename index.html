They are grounded in your own evidence and in sourced case studies from around the world. You leave with a pick, a path and a document you can build on.

What the council is

The Retail Product Council is a multi-agent system that runs inside Claude Code, in a terminal or in VS Code. You bring a problem, an idea or a proposal. A Product Lead orchestrator frames it, checks what the knowledge base already knows, asks you only what it cannot answer, calls the expert panels that matter, makes them challenge each other, and ends with two or three options, one named pick and an iteration path.

This repository is the Retail domain base. It holds what is true across retail: twelve retail areas, retail expert personas, benchmark companies, sourced case studies from every major region, a failure library and a retail glossary. Each client project starts from a copy of it with one command, /start, and adds its own product context.

The problem it solves

What changes when product decisions go through the council.
Without itWith it
Decisions reflect whoever was in the meetingIndependent expert panels, chosen for the question
Retail expertise is scattered across people and projectsThirteen retail expert personas and a shared base every project starts from

Premortems run on imaginationA premortem built from real retail failures
The same mistakes repeatEach correction becomes a rule the system follows next time

What it is not

It does not replace user research. Its simulated users produce hypotheses to test, not findings. It does not own decisions. People do. It is only as good as the context you give it, and it says "not in context" rather than inventing a fact.

How a decision runs

  1. You askA question, a problem, a proposal, or a file to add to the knowledge base.
  2. The council looks first, then asksA scout reads everything your request names and reports what is already known, with sources, for you to correct. Then it asks up to four click-to-answer questions about only the gaps, each with a recommended default.
  3. Panels answer, then ask backEach panel works in its own context and judges the idea for users, the business and stakeholders. It checks how the problem was solved, or failed, elsewhere in the world. Its questions and the figures it needs come back to you once, merged.
  4. Disagreements are forced into the openPanels that conflict see each other's argument and must hold, revise or concede, with evidence.
  5. You get a short decisionTwo or three approaches including doing nothing, one named pick, an iteration path from the smallest viable version, the data that would flip it, and a diagram when the pick changes how something works.
  6. The system learnsYour corrections become rules. Decisions become records whose outcomes you log later with /check outcome.

Panels run as separate subagents on purpose. A panel cannot see another panel's answer, so agreement between two panels means something. Everything passes through the orchestrator, which is the only voice that talks to you.

Failure library

The Premortem tells the story of how an idea failed 12 months after launch. In this council, that story starts from how comparable ventures actually died.

Seven antipatterns

Loot Drop's analysis of about 1,700 failed startups sorts every failure into one of seven antipatterns. In retail and marketplaces two dominate: being out-competed, and unit economics that never worked. Demand is rarely the problem, and marketplaces usually die after early traction, not at launch. The Premortem answers seven self-test questions, one per antipattern, before it writes anything.

The CrushedOutgunned or outspent. About half of all failures.
The Math ProblemEvery sale lost money, at scale.
The BonfireSpent as if revenue were a formality.
The HallucinationBuilt for a customer who did not exist.
The OutlawRegulation was immovable. Kills slowest.
The Lemon and The Civil WarThe product failed, or the team did.

Sixteen retail failure modes

Inside those antipatterns sit sixteen retail mechanisms, each backed by real cases. Examples include scaling before the first market works (Webvan, Getir, TaniHub), automation or warehouses before order density (Kroger and Ocado, Missfresh), subsidised demand mistaken for real demand (Xingsheng Youxuan, Dingdong Maicai, ONDC), a model exported without local fit (Poshmark UK), inflated metrics (Zilingo), and a platform or public rail that removes your role (PayMate after UPI).

Failures by region

Over 40 hand-checked retail failures are grouped by region (China, India, Southeast Asia, Europe and the UK, North America and more), drawn from a classified list of 278 retail failures. A premortem picks its nearest case from the same region as the idea.

Directional, not proof.Loot Drop is AI-assisted and its counts differ between pages. It ranks causes and supplies real cases, but never supplies a number to a verdict.

Knowledge layers

Context engineering is the heart of the council. Knowledge moves through four layers, and each layer has its own rules.

00 SourcesRaw files, exactly as received. Never edited, so you can always go back to what was actually said.
10 ProcessedCleaned transcripts, source reviews and research notes. Evidence, quoted.
20 CuratedAgreed truth: glossary, key facts, decisions, case studies. Changes only with your go.
30 WorkingDrafts, PRDs, epics and stories, built from curated truth.

Read order and cost

Agents read curated first, processed as evidence, and working files only for the task in hand. A self-building index and compact persona cards keep each request cheap. Raw sources are read only during ingestion.

Evidence and conflicts

Every claim is tagged

Verified claims carry the source's own words, for example [V: S-261011-LC "62% of units sold in Q4 2024"]. Inferred claims are tagged [I] with their reasoning. Unknowns are tagged [U]. A hook checks every quote against its file when a session is saved, so an invented quote is caught.

Conflicts stop the work

When two sources disagree, the council stops and asks you which to follow. Your answer goes in the decisions ledger, so the same conflict is never asked twice.

Context before questions

The council never asks you something the knowledge base already answers. It tells you what it knows, with sources, and asks only about the gaps.

Adding knowledge

  1. Drop files in the inboxDocuments, decks, spreadsheets, transcripts, into context/00-sources/inbox/. Or point at a web page.
  2. Type /ingest/ingest context/00-sources/inbox/ registers each source, cleans transcripts, and writes a source review.
  3. Conflicts and duplicates come to youEvery fact is checked against what is already known.
  4. You approve curated changesProposed changes to agreed truth wait for your go. Otherwise the source stays as evidence only.

The learning loop

The council gets better the more you correct it.

1. It producesA decision, a draft or a source review.
2. You correct"That was only a proposal." "Wrong definition."
3. It writes a lessonMistake, root cause and a testable rule. Saved only with your yes.
4. Every session reads itActive lessons load at the start of every session as rules.

A correction that happens twice is counted as a recurrence. /check lesson reviews lessons monthly to promote, merge or retire them. /check outcome records how a past decision turned out, so verdict quality is measured over time.

Behaviour tests

Golden cases test how the council behaves, not just whether files are valid. Retail cases check that it names the region a solution came from, grounds the premortem in real failures, treats a headline as a lead rather than evidence, and never fakes a Jira sync. Start with /start

From a fresh copy to a working council in about ten minutes. You do not edit any file by hand.

  1. Get your own copyOn GitHub click Use this template and clone your new repository, or download the zip and unzip it into a folder named for your project.
  2. Open the folderTerminal: cd into it and run claude. VS Code: File, Open Folder, trust it, then open the Claude panel.
  3. Type /startAnswer a few questions. Click an answer or type your own.
  4. Add what you knowDrop files into context/00-sources/inbox/ and type /ingest context/00-sources/inbox/.
  5. Ask/council Should we add same-day delivery for our top 50 SKUs?

Jira and Confluence

Optional. Not every team uses them, and the council never assumes they are connected.

Without them, decisions, PRDs, epics and stories are saved as files. With them, /draft searches first so it never duplicates a page or ticket, shows the exact changes, and syncs only after your go.

  1. Type /start connectIt adds Atlassian's official server to the project and asks for your Jira project key and Confluence space.
  2. Sign in onceTerminal: run claude again, approve the "atlassian" server if asked, type /mcp, choose atlassian, sign in in the browser. VS Code: open a new conversation, type /mcp, choose atlassian, sign in.
  3. Check/start status shows the Jira row. If sign-in fails, your Atlassian site admin may need to allow the server. Drafts stay local until then.

Contributing and limits

Improving the base

Work on a branch, then open a pull request with /open-pr. Good contributions are new sourced case studies, failures, glossary terms, evidence packs for reference players, and practitioner reviews of the retail experts.

Known limits

  • The retail experts are drafts that no practitioner has checked yet.
  • Reference players have no evidence packs yet, so the competitor panel can only point to case studies.
  • Catalogue, control tower and pricing have no sourced case study yet. Most sources are older than six months.
  • The simulated users are templates until real research fills them.
